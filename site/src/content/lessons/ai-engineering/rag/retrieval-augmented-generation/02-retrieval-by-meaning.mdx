---
title: "Retrieval: finding text by meaning"
description: The retriever turns the question and every chunk into points and keeps the k chunks that lie closest to the question.
order: 2
concepts: [embedding, cosine similarity, top-k retrieval]
---

A customer of the shoe shop types: "Can I send my sneakers back?" The chunk that answers it says: "Items can be returned within 14 days of delivery." The two sentences share no important word, so a search for matching words finds nothing. How does the retriever still pick that chunk?

It compares meaning. An embedding model turns every chunk into an **embedding**, a list of numbers that stands for its meaning, and does the same for the question. Texts with similar meaning get lists that are close together, so every text becomes a point in space.

Below are twelve chunks from the shop's help pages and the question, at illustrative positions. The three highlighted points are what the retriever returns. Drag the question toward the shipping cluster, as if it asked "Where is my parcel?", and watch the list change. Then put it back, raise k to 5, and read the fifth score.

The retriever measured how close the question is to every chunk and kept the closest k. This is **top-k retrieval**, and here k started at 3. The score is **cosine similarity**, which compares the direction of two embeddings: near 1 means almost the same meaning, near 0 means unrelated. At k = 5 the fifth chunk is about parcels, and its score is far below the first four.

**Note.** Real embeddings have hundreds of dimensions, and none has a name such as "returns". Three named axes are a simplification.
